## About OctoAcme Project Management

OctoAcme's project management process follows a clear lifecycle from initiation through retrospective, with lightweight but structured artifacts at each stage. Work begins with a project initiation phase focused on validating business need, defining measurable outcomes, aligning stakeholders, and establishing a go/no-go decision. Once approved, planning emphasizes converting ideas into a prioritized backlog, defining acceptance criteria and Definition of Done, estimating scope, mapping milestones, and identifying risks and dependencies early.

Execution is organized around consistent team rhythms and visible workflow management. Teams use recurring ceremonies—daily or twice-weekly standups, weekly delivery syncs, sprint-end demos/reviews, and monthly stakeholder updates—to keep progress transparent and surface blockers quickly. Work is tracked on a project board (Backlog → Ready → In Progress → In Review → QA → Done), with escalation paths defined from team triage up to PM, Product Lead, and Sponsor when business impact increases. This creates a predictable operating cadence while supporting cross-functional coordination.

Roles and responsibilities are intentionally explicit. Product Managers define problem statements, success metrics, prioritization, and roadmap outcomes; Project Managers coordinate delivery plans, timelines, risks, dependencies, and stakeholder communication; Developers implement and test features, participate in design and review, and highlight technical risk; QA validates acceptance criteria and release readiness; stakeholders provide direction and approvals. Across roles, OctoAcme reinforces principles of customer-first value, iterative delivery, clear ownership, and data-informed decision-making.

Quality assurance and release discipline are embedded throughout delivery rather than treated as end-stage checks. OctoAcme expects unit tests for new logic, integration tests where relevant, end-to-end smoke testing for critical paths, CI-based linting and security scans, and manual QA for feature acceptance as needed. Release readiness also includes documented rollback and mitigation plans, post-deploy verification, and stakeholder communication. After each sprint, release, or incident, retrospectives capture what worked, what needs improvement, and 2–3 prioritized actions with owners and due dates—ensuring continuous improvement is tracked and fed back into the process documentation.
